# Presentation script: Matthew Brady

Fourth draft, 26 October 2026, for the dry run. Written from the slides as they stand in presentation/slides.json (Willis's final edits, 23 October). It is a script to practise from, not to read: once it feels natural, say it in your own words and look at the room, not the page.

The audience is i.c.stars staff. This is the sprint 2 presentation, and its one job is to prove the pipeline is built and working. Slide 12 gives a quick, honest status of the extra features: what's built and being refined, and what's still planned.

**The script never reads the slide.** Each slide starts with an "On screen" line, so you can see what the audience is already reading. What you say adds the story, the reason or the example behind it, in different words. Let the slide carry the headline; you carry the meaning.

Your parts: the intro and slides 1 to 5 (about 3 minutes), then slide 12 "Additional features" and slide 13, the close (about 1¼ minutes).

## Slide 2: Pipeline built (about 25 seconds)

> On screen: "Pipeline built", ten dots lighting up, "Every question now runs end to end, through all ten stages."

This sprint had one goal, and that's it up there.

Think of it as the plumbing behind Isa. Last sprint, we had good pipes lying on the workbench. Now they're fitted together, and the water runs: a leader pours a question in at one end, and it flows all the way through until it comes out as an answer, or gets piped to a real person. ## Slide 3: The problem (about 35 seconds)

> On screen: a big "6", "people on the support team", "Chapter leaders are volunteers. They need approved guidance fast, and a six-person support team cannot answer every question." Two panels: "Isa answers from approved content." and "Isa hands off to a person when it should."

So why build this at all?

Picture yourself as a chapter president. You've got a full-time job, it's nine at night, and you need to know an election rule before tomorrow's board meeting.

*(Point to the 6.)* Now look at who you can call. That's the whole team, for chapters all over the world. The math just doesn't work.

*(Point to the two panels.)* So Isa splits the work. If the answer is already written down in the client's documents, she gives it to you, right then. If it needs a human being, she makes sure it gets to one.

She's there to take the pressure off that team, not to take their place.

**[CLICK]**

## Slide 4: What Isa promises (about 35 seconds)

> On screen: "What Isa promises", "Three rules, shown on every answer." Three cards: Approved content only; Isa shows its work; A person takes over.

These three are what make Isa trustworthy.

*(First card.)* If it isn't in the client's own documents, Isa won't say it. No guessing, no internet, and every answer tells you which page it came from.

*(Second card.)* Nothing happens behind a curtain. A leader can open up any answer and follow exactly how Isa got there.

*(Third card.)* And Isa knows her limits. When she can't help, she says so, passes the question on, and the leader walks away with a number to quote when they follow up.

**[CLICK]**

## Slide 5: One question, ten stages (about 50 seconds)

> On screen: "One question, ten stages", the ten stage names lighting up in order, and three bars: Cheap path, AI path, To a person.

*Let the stages light up for a moment before you speak.*

Let's follow one question through.

It arrives. Isa checks who's asking and makes sure it's something she should be answering. She goes looking in the client's documents. Before anything goes back to the leader, she checks her own work. And if a person needs to step in, she opens a ticket for them.

*(Point to the blue bar.)* Here's the clever part. Most questions are easy: the answer is sitting right there in the documents, word for word, so Isa just hands it over. The AI never even wakes up.

*(Point to the dark blue bar.)* The trickier ones, where the answer is spread across a few pages, take the longer road. That's where the AI pieces it together, and only from what Isa found.

*(Point to the green bar.)* And on either road, if it needs a human, it ends with one.

Enough from me. Willis is going to show you what's actually working, and then let Isa prove it live.

*Hand over to Willis. Don't click: slide 6 is his.*

## If you're running long

- Slide 3: drop the "Picture yourself..." story and start at "Point to the 6".
- Slide 5: skip the walk through the stages; keep the three bars.
- Slide 12: say only "The two on the left work today and we're still polishing them; the right side is planned," and click on.

## Remember

- Say "the client", never the client's name. The slides do the same.
- No model timing (how many seconds an answer takes).
- Say "planned" for anything not built.
- Don't restart Isa during the talk: ticket numbers start again at TKT-0001.
- The slide labels onboarding and the notebook "In the build, not finished". Saying they "work in the app today" and "we're still polishing them" matches that.
