## Slide 11: "Next steps"

**On screen:** "In priority order." Seven numbered items: real translation, Salesforce tickets, real input rules, vector search and RAG, knowledge graph, output compliance, measuring quality.

**Points to get across:**
- These are in priority order, and most of them turn the placeholders Willis showed into the real thing.
- **Translation first,** because chapter leaders work in many languages. The plan is a self-hosted engine (LibreTranslate / Argos), plus a second self-hosted model for languages it doesn't cover, such as Amharic, Oromo, Somali and Tigrinya. Self-hosted means the client's material never leaves their systems.
- **Salesforce tickets:** the connection is already written; it just needs to pass its live test before it's switched on. Then hand-offs land directly in the support team's own system.
- **Real input rules:** replace today's simple keyword check with proper rules for what Isa should and shouldn't take on.
- **Vector search:** find passages by meaning more accurately than today's simpler approach.
- **Knowledge graph:** link related sections, so Isa can point to "you may also need…".
- **Output compliance:** add the deeper checks that the answer is really supported by its source, plus a confidence score.
- **Measuring quality:** regularly sample answers for accuracy, and let leaders give feedback, so the team knows Isa is getting better, not just bigger.

**Say it differently, for example:** "Everything Willis called a stub has a plan behind it. The first job is making sure a leader can ask in their own language, without their question ever leaving the client's own servers."

## Slide 12: "Additional features"

**On screen:** two groups. "In the build, not finished": onboarding (the Today panel and first-90-days journey) and the succession notebook. "Planned": peer content panel, offline packages, Android app wrapper, Chrome extension, multi-model testing.

**Points to get across:**
- **In the build, not finished:**
  - Onboarding: a new chapter leader gets steps matched to where they are in their term, with their progress, so their first months aren't a guessing game.
  - Succession notebook: chapter leadership changes every year, and knowledge walks out the door. The notebook lets a leader leave notes for whoever takes over next.
- **Planned:**
  - A peer content panel alongside the chat, so leaders can learn from what other chapters do.
  - Offline packages for remote chapters with unreliable internet.
  - An Android app wrapper: Isa as an app icon on a phone, using the same server; nothing new to maintain.
  - A Chrome extension: a quick way into the chat for leaders who live in their browser.
  - Side-by-side model testing for the team, to compare AI models before choosing one.

**Say it differently, for example:** "The pipeline answers questions. These features are about the person asking: the new leader in their first week, the one handing over to a successor, and the one in a chapter where the internet drops out."
