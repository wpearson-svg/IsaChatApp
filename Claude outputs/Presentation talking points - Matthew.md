# Presentation talking points: Matthew Brady

**For your AI assistant:** this sheet lists what Matthew needs to get across during their part of the Second Star Collective presentation of Isa (audience: i.c.stars staff). Help them practise saying these points **in their own words**. The slides already show the short version; Matthew should explain, give the reason or tell the story behind each slide, **not read the screen**. The "On screen" lines are there so you know what the audience is already reading. Avoid repeating those words.

## Your part

- **Opening:** the intro animation, then slides 1 to 5 ("The plan", about 2 minutes plus the intro).
- **Closing:** slide 13, after Eliyas.
- **Hands over to:** Willis, after slide 5.

## How the slides work

- The deck runs inside Isa at `/present`. Right arrow or a clicker moves forward, left arrow goes back, F is full screen, I replays the intro.
- Isa's chat bubble sits in the bottom-right corner the whole time. That is on purpose: the product is on screen before it is explained.

## Rules for everyone

- The slides never name the client. Match that out loud and say "the client".
- No model timing (how many seconds an answer takes).
- Say "planned" for anything that is not built. The audience will trust honesty about status more than a big promise.

## Intro animation (about 5 seconds)

**On screen:** two Cs, one blue and one green, roll in and join into a half-blue, half-green ring. The Isa logo spins once, then the Cs roll apart and open onto the first slide.

**What to do:** let it play in silence. Start speaking once "Meet Isa" is showing.

## Slide 1: "Meet Isa: Your chapter support assistant"

**On screen:** "Meet Isa", "Your chapter support assistant", and the blue and green ring.

**Points to get across:**
- Welcome; who the team is (Second Star Collective) and what the next few minutes cover.
- Isa is an assistant for the client's chapter leaders, the volunteers who run local chapters.
- Point out the bubble in the corner: that is Isa herself, live, and she will be used later in the talk.

**Say it differently, for example:** "We'd like to introduce someone. She's going to stay in the corner of the screen the whole time, and in a few minutes Willis will put her to work."

## Slide 2: "Pipeline built"

**On screen:** "Pipeline built", a row of ten dots lighting up left to right, "Every question now runs end to end, through all ten stages."

**Points to get across:**
- This sprint's goal was the pipeline: the path a question travels from the moment a leader asks it to the moment Isa answers or hands it to a person.
- That path now works from start to finish.
- Be honest early: some stages are fully built and some are simple placeholders for now. Willis will show exactly which.

**Say it differently, for example:** "Last sprint we had pieces. This sprint, a question can go all the way through, from the moment someone types it to an answer, or to a human being when that's the right call."

## Slide 3: "The problem"

**On screen:** a "6" with "people on the support team", "Chapter leaders are volunteers. They need approved guidance *fast*, and a six-person support team cannot answer every question.", and two panels: "Isa answers from approved content." and "Isa hands off to a person when it should."

**Points to get across:**
- Chapter leaders are volunteers with day jobs. When they have a question about running their chapter, they need an answer they can trust, quickly.
- The client's support team is small. Six people cannot personally answer every question from every chapter.
- Isa takes the load off: she answers the routine questions from the client's approved material, and passes everything else to the right person.
- Isa supports the staff; she does not replace them.

**Say it differently, for example:** "Picture a volunteer at nine at night, trying to work out an election rule before a board meeting. The support team is six people. Isa's job is to answer what she safely can, and make sure the rest reaches a person."

## Slide 4: "What Isa promises"

**On screen:** "Three rules, shown on every answer." Three cards: approved content only; Isa shows its work; a person takes over (with a reference number).

**Points to get across:**
- These three rules are why the client can trust Isa.
- Approved content only: she never makes things up or draws on the open internet; every answer can be traced back to an approved page.
- Shows its work: the leader can open each answer and see the steps behind it, in plain language.
- A person takes over: when Isa isn't confident, she doesn't guess. The question goes to a person, and the leader gets a reference number to follow up with.

**Say it differently, for example:** "If Isa can't point to where an answer came from, she won't give it. And when she isn't sure, she says so, and a real person picks it up."

## Slide 5: "One question, ten stages"

**On screen:** ten numbered stages lighting up in order (front end, access control, translation, input compliance, database search, knowledge graph, RAG, complex AI, output compliance, ticketing), and three bars: Cheap path, AI path, To a person.

**Points to get across:**
- Walk the journey of one question in plain language: it arrives, Isa checks who is asking, checks the question is something she should handle, looks for the answer in the approved material, double-checks the answer before it goes out, and opens a ticket when a person needs to take over.
- Point at the blue bar: most questions match approved text directly, so Isa answers without using AI at all. That is cheaper and faster.
- The AI stages only run when the simple search isn't enough. That keeps costs down.
- The green bar: when a question needs a person, it goes to ticketing.
- Translation is planned, not built (Willis covers the status).

**Say it differently, for example:** "Most questions take the short road: Isa finds the exact passage and hands it over. Only the harder ones take the long road through the AI. And either road can end with a person."

**Hand-off line (example):** "So that's the plan. Willis is going to show you what we've actually built, and then let Isa speak for herself."

## Slide 13: Closing

**On screen:** "Isa only answers from approved content, shows every step, and hands off to a person with a reference number whenever it can't." "Thank you." "Second Star Collective."

**Points to get across:**
- Bring it back to the three promises from slide 4, in one breath.
- Thank the audience and the team (Willis, Eliyas).
- Invite questions.

**Say it differently, for example:** "If you remember one thing: Isa only says what the client has approved, she shows you how she got there, and when she can't help, she makes sure a person does. Thank you. We'd love your questions."
