# Best Practice Guide: Board and Officer Roles

General governance best practice for volunteer chapter boards, written by the Isa team in its own words from the public guidance listed under Further reading. It is not ISACA policy: ISACA's role guidebooks and your chapter's bylaws define the roles in your chapter, and they come first.

## What the board as a whole does

A board's job is oversight and direction, not running day-to-day activities. Its main responsibilities are to keep the mission in view, plan and then check progress against the plan, make sure programs serve the mission, protect the chapter's money and assets through a budget and financial controls, recruit and develop good board members, and keep the chapter legally and ethically sound. Each board member also owes the chapter care (decide carefully and with good information), loyalty (put the chapter's interests ahead of their own) and faithfulness to the mission and the rules.

## What every board member is expected to do

Every board member should attend board meetings, read the agenda and materials before each meeting, serve on at least one committee or take on a special task, keep up with the chapter's activities and policies, follow the conflict of interest and confidentiality policies, help find future leaders, and speak well of the chapter. Board members share responsibility for board decisions, including ones they voted against, and should disagree respectfully in the meeting rather than outside it.

## The president or chair

The president (or board chair) leads the board as a team. Typical duties are to plan the agenda and run board meetings, make sure every member can take part, appoint committee chairs and suggest committee members, help with new member orientation, act as the board's main spokesperson, lead a regular board self-assessment, and mentor the person who will take over. The president has the same single vote as other members; some presidents speak last so they don't steer the discussion.

## The vice president

The vice president (or vice chair) is the backup leader, so the board is never without someone to lead. Typical duties are to run meetings when the president is absent, take on special assignments from the president, share leadership tasks, and prepare to step into the president's role.

## The secretary

The secretary looks after the board's records. Typical duties are to send meeting notices, record attendance and whether a quorum was present, take or review the minutes and bring them to the next meeting for approval, keep board records, documents and contracts safe and in one place, and keep track of board members' terms. Any board member can take minutes if needed, but the secretary makes sure they are complete and filed.

## The treasurer

The treasurer looks after the chapter's money on behalf of the board. Typical duties are to prepare or oversee the annual budget and present it for approval, make sure board members receive clear, timely financial reports, chair any finance committee, help the board understand the chapter's financial position, and work with whoever reviews or audits the accounts. A treasurer needs some understanding of basic accounting and financial statements.

## Terms, job descriptions and holding more than one role

Written descriptions of each board role help everyone know what is expected, and give the board something to refer back to later. A role description usually covers the role's duties, who it reports to, the term dates, the expected time commitment, and the skills it needs. Many boards limit how many terms in a row a person can serve, and stagger terms so the whole board never changes at once. It is usually better for one person to hold only one officer role. Your chapter's bylaws set the actual terms and limits.
