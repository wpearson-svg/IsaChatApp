# Best Practice Guide: Recruiting and Engaging Volunteers

General governance best practice for volunteer chapter boards, written by the Isa team in its own words from the public guidance listed under Further reading. It is not ISACA policy: ISACA's documents set any requirements for volunteers, such as signed volunteer agreements.

## Finding new volunteers and board members

The most effective way to recruit a volunteer is to ask them personally. Look at the skills and backgrounds the board already has, note the gaps, and look for people who fill them, at events, through committees and through members' networks. Explain to each candidate why their skills would help and what the role involves before they say yes. Keep a list of possible future leaders all year, not only at election time.

## Setting clear expectations for board members

Agree on what the chapter expects of every board member and tell new and returning members the same thing, ideally in writing. Typical expectations are to attend meetings regularly, arrive having read the materials, take part in at least one committee, use personal and professional contacts to help the chapter, and raise disagreements respectfully in the meeting. When members have different ideas about their role, the board stops working as a team.

## Keeping volunteers engaged

Learn what motivates each volunteer and give them work that uses their skills and interests. Give volunteers one clear person to contact, tell them how much time a task needs, and respect their limits to avoid burnout. Pair newer volunteers with experienced ones so knowledge is passed on. Ask volunteers for feedback, and act on it, so they feel like partners rather than helpers. When someone steps down, ask what would have made the role better.

## Recognizing and thanking volunteers

Thank volunteers often and publicly, for example at chapter events, in newsletters or in the minutes. Small gestures, such as a certificate, a mention at the annual meeting or a personal note, build loyalty. Recognition also shows other members that volunteering is valued, and it helps move strong volunteers toward leadership roles.

## Improving how the board works

Make board effectiveness a regular topic, for example once a year during planning. A short self-assessment survey every year or two helps members see where the board is strong and where it needs work. Offer training on the topics members ask for, such as reading financial statements, running meetings, meeting rules, the bylaws and strategic planning, and keep past agendas, minutes and budgets in one place so everyone can find them.

## Welcoming new chapter members

New members are more likely to stay when someone connects with them early. Contact each new member personally within their first month, ideally from a fellow volunteer rather than an automated message, and check in again around six months. Send a short series of welcome messages over the first 90 days, each focused on one way to get involved, and offer easy first steps such as a local meetup or an online session.
