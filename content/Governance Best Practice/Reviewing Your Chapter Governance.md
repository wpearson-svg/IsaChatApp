# Best Practice Guide: Reviewing Your Chapter Governance

General governance best practice for volunteer chapter boards, written by the Isa team in its own words from the public guidance listed under Further reading. It is not ISACA policy. Changing your bylaws must follow your bylaws' own amendment rules and ISACA's requirements for chapters: ask ISACA's Chapter Experience team before you change them.

## When to review how the board is set up

Consider a review of the board's structure when the same problems keep coming back: members aren't engaged, decisions take too long, roles overlap, or the bylaws no longer match how the chapter actually works. Reviewing the bylaws every few years, through a short-term committee, keeps them up to date.

## Agree on the problem before the solution

Changes to how a board is set up work best when people first agree on what the problems are. Start by writing down what good governance should achieve for the chapter, then compare that with how things work today and list the gaps. Only then design options to close the gaps. Jumping straight to a solution, such as "we need a smaller board", tends to split people into camps.

## Steps for a governance review

A practical sequence is: form a small review group that includes past leaders and some critical voices; agree what the board should achieve; list the gaps; have the board approve the gaps; design and compare several options; share the preferred option with members and listen to their concerns; refine it; have the board approve it; update the bylaws to match; and plan the changeover so the chapter keeps running. Keep detailed procedures in board policies rather than in the bylaws, so they can be updated more easily.

## Pitfalls to avoid in a governance review

Allow enough time, since agreement builds slowly. Talk often with influential members, but don't circulate a proposal before it's ready. Remember that board members serve the whole chapter, not one group within it. Aim for a decision everyone can live with, rather than one everyone loves.

## The board's role in planning

Setting the chapter's direction is the board's job. Set aside dedicated time for planning at least once a year, keep the plan short and adaptable, and review progress against it regularly, for example every quarter. Use the plan to decide which projects to take on and which to decline.
