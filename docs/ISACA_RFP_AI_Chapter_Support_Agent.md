REQUEST FOR PROPOSAL

AI-Enabled Chapter Knowledge and Support Agent

RFP Issue Date: September 1, 2026
Proposal Due: September 4, 2026

RFP intent. AI-enabled chapter support agent.

Key principle: Help chapter leaders find and understand ISACA-approved information while supplementing human Chapter Experience support.

## 1. Background and Business Need

ISACA pioneers teh workforce by empowering its community with the knowledge, credentials, training and network they need to thrive in fields like information security, governance, assurance, risk management, data privacy and emerging tech across more than 195 countries and with more than 230 chapters worldwide of Chapter Operations and Program staff, and Chapter Success Managers (chapters being essentially small associations).

Therefore, ISACA is seeking a services that provides timely, consistent access to approved chapter governance, operations, program, product, and related supportinformation while preserving staff involvement for matters requiring judgment, interpretation, mediation, escalation, or exception handling.

The proposed agent should help ISACA:

- Govern: access and understanding approved chapter guidance.
- Automate: Reduce avoidable or repetitive information requests where self-service
- Interface: Provide consistency in how approved information is surfaced to chapter leaders
- Redirect: Direct users to human support only when questions are out of scope
- Adminable: Establish a sustainable, scaleable governance framework.

## 2. Scope of Work

Respondents should propose a phased approach. ISACA expects approval gates between major phases and may authorize only selected phases. The vendor should recommend sequencing, duration, dependencies, and decision criteria.

| Phase | Vendor Focus | Expected Outputs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Discovery & Requirements | Stakeholders, chapter-user needs, support patterns, candidate use cases, knowledge landscape, constraints, risks, accessibility/security/privacy considerations, pilot readiness. | Discovery findings; prioritized use cases; requirements; assumptions/dependencies; recommended pilot scope. |
| 2. Knowledge & Governance Design | Inventory and assess candidate knowledge sources; define approval, ownership, lifecycle, permissions, conflict handling, and controls against unauthorized content. | Approved pilot knowledge inventory; governance model; content lifecycle and permissions approach. |
| 3. Solution Design & Prototype | Recommend conceptual architecture and user experience; build/configure a testable MVP using approved scope and content; implement source behavior, uncertainty, and escalation. | Conceptual solution design; working prototype/MVP; configuration/design documentation. |
| 4. Testing & Pilot | Test answer quality, grounding, source use, uncertainty, escalation, usability, accessibility, security/privacy considerations, and issue remediation; launch an approved pilot. | Test results; issue log; readiness assessment; operational pilot; usage/feedback data. |
| 5. Evaluation & Production Option | Analyze pilot results; refine as approved; recommend production approach, operating model, support, maintenance, knowledge transfer, and transition if ISACA proceeds. | Pilot evaluation; refinement backlog; production recommendation/options; implementation and support plan if approved. |

## 3. Required Solution Capabilities and Controls

Proposals should explain how the recommended approach would address the following, at a level appropriate for an RFP response:

- **Approved-content grounding:** Responses should be based on ISACA-approved knowledge sources only. The vendor should describe how approved sources are identified, prepared, connected, updated, and retired.
- **Answer quality and hallucination mitigation:** Describe controls for unsupported responses, uncertainty handling, source transparency, pre-launch testing, post-launch monitoring, and remediation.
- **Content governance:** Address ownership, approval permissions, versioning/currency, duplicate or conflicting content, and prevention of unauthorized content from influencing answers.
- **Escalation and human handoff:** Explain how the agent will recognize questions it should not answer and route or direct users toward appropriate human support.
- **User experience:** Recommend an intuitive experience for volunteer chapter leaders across a global network, including how users ask questions, understand answers, see uncertainty, and access source information where appropriate.
- **Security and privacy:** Describe data handling, retention, access controls, protection of confidential/restricted information, third-party risks, security testing, issue response, and dependencies on ISACA security/privacy/legal input.
- **Accessibility:** Describe the approach to meeting applicable accessibility requirements once finalized by ISACA.
- **Analytics and measurement:** Recommend metrics and reporting for adoption, usefulness, answer quality, escalation, knowledge currency, user experience, and operational impact.
- **Maintainability and scalability:** Describe how the solution can be maintained, monitored, governed, and scaled across ISACA's global chapter network, including language support considerations if required later.

## 4. AI Use in Delivery and Development

The respondent must disclose any use of artificial intelligence, machine learning, generative AI, coding assistants, or AI-enabled third-party services in performing the engagement or creating project deliverables. This requirement applies to source code, configurations, prompts, test cases, analyses, designs, documentation, training materials, reports, knowledge content, and other work products.

The selected vendor must maintain an AI-use register identifying, at minimum:

- the AI tool, provider, model, utilized;
- the purpose and project activity for which it was used;
- the information submitted to the tool;
- the resulting deliverable or artifact;
- whether human review and validation occurred;

## 8. Evaluation Criteria

ISACA will evaluate the proposal using the following factors.

| Evaluation Area | What ISACA May Consider |
| --- | --- |
| Business understanding and discovery approach | How well the proposal frames the support scalability challenge and uncovers user, content, governance, risk, and decision needs. |
| Solution design and feasibility | Practicality, scalability, maintainability, phasing, dependencies, and avoidance of premature technology lock-in. |
| Knowledge governance and answer-quality controls | Strength of grounding, hallucination mitigation, source handling, content controls, testing, monitoring, and escalation. |
| User experience, accessibility, security, and privacy | Quality of the proposed user experience and risk-control approach. |
| Pilot and measurement approach | Ability to test the concept, define readiness, measure outcomes, and support evidence-based go/no-go decisions. |
| Vendor experience and team | Relevant experience, qualifications, roles, and ability to execute the engagement. |
| Knowledge transfer and sustainability | Ability to enable ISACA to govern, maintain, understand, and sustain the approved solution model. |
| Commercial clarity | Transparency of pricing, assumptions, recurring costs, optional costs, and cost drivers. |
